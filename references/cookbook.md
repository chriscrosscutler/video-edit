# Cookbook: recipes per content type

Read when planning a video of one of these kinds. Each recipe gives the beats, the footage to
ask for, and the tools. Lengths are for short-form; plan.md has the general structure and the
filming guide. Every recipe works in the calm house style; "energetic" notes what to add when the
person wants pace.

## Haul

What came, one item at a time, with a verdict each.

- **Open:** all items at once (a table spread, the bag tipped out) with the count: "6 things I
  got this week". A `ListCounter` holds the count in a corner.
- **Per item (5-10 s):** name it on the cut to its footage (see and say), one reason, a verdict.
  Hands-only B-roll of each item; the talking take covers the reasons.
- **End:** the favourite, then the CTA.
- Energetic: a `Sticker` per verdict, a pop sound as each item lands, a ramp through unpacking.

## Unboxing

The reveal is the payoff, so it plays in real time.

- **Open:** the finished product in use for 1-2 s, then "here's what's in the box".
- **Body:** the outside of the box, the opening, each layer. Long cutting of tape: a speed ramp
  (energetic) or cut to the moment the lid lifts (calm). The first look at the product plays whole,
  with its own sound.
- **Close-ups:** the details she names, with a `Zoom` digital zoom or a `Callout`.
- **Privacy:** the shipping label is on the box; blur it (render.md) before anything else.

## Get ready with me (GRWM)

A routine told while doing it; the talking and the doing are the same take.

- **Open:** the finished look first, then cut to the start: "getting ready for ...".
- **Body:** each step plays as it happens. The story she tells carries the video; the products are
  named as she reaches for them, with the product held to camera for 2 s.
- Long steps (blending, curling) play as interludes or are shortened by dropping lines, never sped
  up in the calm style.
- **End:** the finished look again, held, then the CTA.
- Shoot locked off, one light, exposure locked; a mirror shot needs the phone out of the reflection.

## Review

One product, an honest verdict.

- **Open:** the verdict or the surprising fact: "I didn't expect to like this".
- **Body:** what it is (5 s), what's good (with footage of it working), what's not, who it's for.
  Keep the negatives: they make the positives believable.
- **Proof:** a before and after (`SplitScreen mode="wipe"`), a timer, a measurement on screen.
- **End:** the verdict again in one line, the CTA.
- Sponsored: the disclosure at the start, before any praise (compliance.md).

## Comparison

Two or more products against the same tests.

- **Open:** both side by side (`SplitScreen`) and the question: "$15 vs $60: which one".
- **Body:** one test per beat, both products in the same test, the same framing and light. A label
  on each pane; the winner of each beat said aloud.
- **End:** the overall winner and why, then who should buy the other one.
- Match the two takes' colour (`match.py`) so the light is not what decides.

## Tutorial

Say, show, result, per step.

- **Open:** the finished result, then "how to ... in N steps".
- **Per step:** a `ChapterCard` (lower) or `ListCounter`, the line, the action in full, the result
  of the step. Never skip showing a result.
- **Screens:** a screen recording in a `PhoneMockup`, or full frame with a `Callout` on the button.
- **End:** the result again, and the one mistake to avoid.
- A long tutorial cut down: `clips.py` finds the steps that stand alone.

## Talking head, story or opinion

- **Open:** the line that makes the point, not a greeting.
- **Body:** lines in order, cutaways on what she names, a punch-in to hide a jump cut. Two phones
  (cut.md) give a second angle for the cuts.
- **End:** the payoff line, held a beat; the CTA as text.
